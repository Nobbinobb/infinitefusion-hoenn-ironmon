# Automated single-Pokemon pivot system

## Purpose

Ironmon enforces its pivot rules in-game instead of relying on the player to
remember voluntary restrictions. The player owns one party Pokemon. Required
story gifts and trades use short-lived generated Pokemon rather than permanent
party exceptions. Obtaining an eligible Pokemon starts an immediate, blind
pivot that ends with exactly one usable Pokemon.

The system is intended to make every catch a commitment while preserving
fusion as a high-risk, seed-consistent gamble. It must not permit repeated
fusion, reversal, or unfusion chains that turn every encounter into a tedious
optimization exercise.

## Core invariants

- The usable party contains at most one Pokemon.
- Temporary progression Pokemon never survive their required story transaction
  or count as usable party members.
- HM rewards become their corresponding permanent field tools, so field-move
  progression never requires a spare party Pokemon.
- An eligible acquisition must be resolved before normal play continues.
- A completed pivot leaves exactly one usable Pokemon.
- The previous Pokemon and every unused result are permanently discarded.
- A successful acquisition cannot be cancelled after its pivot choices appear.
- Pivot fusion, reversal, and unfusion actions are free and do not use bag
  items.
- Fusion and reversal items cannot bypass the pivot system while Ironmon is
  active.
- No pivot transformation can be chained into another transformation.

The operation is transactional. Ironmon must not discard the current Pokemon
until the selected result has been created and validated successfully.

## Blind-decision boundary

Until the pivot is committed, the player cannot open the new Pokemon's summary
or inspect its stats, moves, ability, or nature. Information that appeared
naturally during the encounter remains legal; the system does not conceal a
move or ability that the player already observed in battle.

Normal inspection resumes immediately after the chosen result becomes the
single usable party Pokemon.

## Pivot actions

| Current Pokemon | Newly obtained Pokemon | Available actions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| None | Normal | Take |
| None | Fusion | Take, reverse and take, or unfuse and take one component |
| Normal | Normal | Swap or fuse |
| Fusion | Normal | Swap |
| Any | Fusion | Swap, swap and reverse, or swap and unfuse |

Actions have the following effects:

- **Take/Swap:** install the new Pokemon and permanently discard the previous
  Pokemon, if any.
- **Fuse:** consume the current and new normal Pokemon, generate their mapped
  fusion result, and install that result.
- **Swap and reverse:** reverse a newly obtained fusion once, install the
  reversed result, and discard the previous Pokemon.
- **Swap and unfuse:** split a newly obtained fusion, resolve one eligible
  component according to the configured selection rule, install it, and
  discard the other component and previous Pokemon.

If the current Pokemon is fused and the new Pokemon is normal, swap is the only
legal action. Unfusing the current Pokemon to enable fusion would be a forbidden
chain. A newly obtained fusion cannot itself be fused with the current Pokemon.

## Fusion gamble mapping

Fusion maps an unordered pair of normal input species to two results from the
validated custom-sprite fusion pool. Normal Pokemon, autogenerated fusions,
and triple or special fusions cannot be selected as results. There is no BST
restriction; the outcome is intentionally a high-risk, high-reward gamble.

The mapping is derived from the generator schema version, run seed, a dedicated
player-fusion namespace, and the canonical unordered input pair. It must not
depend on call order or the individual Pokemon's personal ID.

Orientation selects one member of the shared result pair:

- `A body + B head` produces mapped result `C`.
- `B body + A head` produces mapped result `D`.
- Ironmon reversal maps `C` to `D` and `D` to `C`.

`C` and `D` are selected from the full custom-sprite pool; neither depends on
the game's natural reversed sprite being custom. The pool is arranged into
deterministic, seed-specific reverse pairs whose members share no component
species, so caught randomized fusions use the same two-way reversal rule.
Every ordered A+B pair therefore produces the same result throughout one run.
Save/load preserves the mapping, while F7 creates a new run seed and new
mappings. Two different input pairs may map to the same result.

An undiscovered result remains hidden until fusion is committed. After the
pair has been used successfully, its discovered result may be shown for later
fusions of the same pair. Inspection and diagnostics intended for ordinary
players must not reveal undiscovered mappings.

## Fusion origin and transformation rights

Fusions need an origin marker so item handlers and other game paths can enforce
the rules:

- **Player-created fusion:** produced by the fusion gamble and permanently
  ineligible for reversal or unfusion.
- **Caught fusion:** eligible for exactly one immediate pivot outcome: keep,
  reverse, or unfuse.
- **Processed caught fusion:** already committed by one of those outcomes and
  permanently ineligible for later reversal or unfusion.

Keeping a caught fusion unchanged also closes its transformation opportunity.
Reverse followed by unfuse, unfuse followed by fuse, and any later use of a DNA
Reverser or DNA Splicer are prohibited.

## Unfusion component setting

Ironmon configuration adds one stable setting with two values:

- `:random_component` (**default**): commit first, then select and reveal one
  component deterministically.
- `:player_choice`: show the two component identities and let the player choose
  one without inspecting either summary.

Random component selection is derived from the run seed and a stable identifier
for that acquisition. Reloading before the pivot must select the same component,
while F7 may select a different component in the new run. The selection is
independent of head/body orientation so reversal cannot manipulate it.

In both modes, the unselected component is permanently discarded.

## Data ownership after fusion

For a mapped result C composed of X and Y:

- Species, sprite, types, base stats, future level-up data, evolutions, and
  available ability slots belong to C and its displayed X/Y components.
- Level, experience, IVs, EVs, shininess, ownership, and held-item handling
  follow the normal fusion procedure unless a later design explicitly changes
  them.
- Existing move selection and nature selection continue to use the two consumed
  Pokemon unless their later randomizers define a replacement rule.

Ability randomization must therefore derive C's available abilities from the
randomized abilities of X and Y, not from the sacrificed A and B. Directly
encountered copies of C must expose the same run-consistent ability slots.

## Acquisition coverage

The forced pivot applies to permanent wild catches, static Pokemon, gifts,
hatched Pokemon, and duplicate Pokemon created by evolution. Required trades
generate a temporary outgoing Pokemon that satisfies the trade rule without
consuming the player's current Pokemon. The received Pokemon then enters the
normal forced-pivot flow.

Eggs remain excluded while unhatched and may move through storage. Hatching
immediately removes the Egg exclusion and starts a normal forced pivot before
play resumes. Temporary rentals remain explicitly excluded and are not treated
as permanent acquisitions. Day Care deposit is blocked because it would store
the sole usable Pokemon outside the protected party and PC paths.

Every acquisition path must be intercepted before a new Pokemon is sent to
ordinary PC storage. PC withdrawal and other storage paths must not allow the
one-Pokemon invariant to be bypassed.

## Forms and special cases

- Mechanically distinct species/forms receive distinct mappings.
- Cosmetic forms share their base species mapping.
- Same-species input pairs are valid and receive one deterministic result.
- Triple and special fusions are excluded from the gamble pool until explicitly
  designed and tested.
- Cosmetic forms follow the game's shared base-species identity; mechanically
  distinct species/forms retain their own source mapping where the game assigns
  them a distinct species ID.
- Battle facilities and temporary challenge teams retain their original
  restoration lifecycle. Utility Pokemon fail able-team eligibility and are
  removed from the battle-facing party if a temporary selector includes them.

## Acceptance criteria

- Every covered acquisition opens the correct pivot actions and ends with one
  usable Pokemon.
- No summary data can be inspected before commitment.
- Swapping, fusing, reversing, and unfusing discard every ineligible Pokemon.
- The same ordered input pair maps consistently within a run and changes only
  with a new seed.
- Opposite input orientation produces the other custom result, and Ironmon
  reversal swaps those results in both directions.
- Unknown results are not revealed before the first committed fusion.
- Player-created and processed fusions cannot be reversed or unfused later.
- Both unfusion component settings behave as documented and survive save/load
  and F7 configuration preservation.
- Reloading cannot reroll a random unfusion component.
- PC, item, gift, static, trade, and story paths cannot create an extra usable
  party Pokemon or bypass a pending pivot.
- Required gifts and trades cannot consume the current Pokemon or leave a
  generated progression Pokemon in the party.
- A failed pivot transaction preserves the current Pokemon and reports a clear
  error.
